Design

Design

Algorithms and sources: QUADPACK, the Runge–Kutta methods, Radau, BDF and the stiffness switch, and where the code departs from them.

You are reading the documentation of an outdated version, whose text may no longer be accurate. Go to the latest version.

integrate provides what scipy.integrate provides for quadrature and initial value problems, in pure Go, with the same algorithms, defaults and diagnostics. Where an algorithm is a port, it follows its reference line by line, so that a run here and a run of the reference on the same problem take the same steps; verification.md measures how far that holds.

Quadrature

QUADPACK (Quad)

Source: R. Piessens, E. de Doncker-Kapenga, C. W. Überhuber, D. K. Kahaner, QUADPACK, A Subroutine Package for Automatic Integration, Springer 1983; the double-precision Fortran at netlib.org/quadpack.

The translation keeps the Fortran’s 1-based arrays, constants (d1mach values), loop bounds and the order in which subintervals are bisected and extrapolated; the goto structure becomes for/break/continue with the labels named in comments. QuadResult carries QUADPACK’s result, abserr, neval and last; ier becomes a *QuadError wrapping one sentinel per code (1 ErrLimit, 2 ErrRoundoff, 3 ErrBadIntegrand, 4 ErrExtrapolation, 5 ErrDivergent, 6 ErrInvalidInput, 7 ErrCycles). For codes other than 6 the estimate is still returned, as SciPy returns it with an IntegrationWarning.

The dispatch is SciPy’s quad: limits are swapped (and the result negated) when b < a, the routine is chosen from the limits and the weight, break points are de-duplicated and restricted to the open interval, a cos/sin weight on (-∞, b] integrates f(-x) on [-b, ∞). SciPy’s wvar is split into typed fields (Omega, Alpha/Beta, C). EpsAbs/EpsRel default to 1.49e-8; a negative value switches a criterion off (SciPy’s epsabs=0).

Deliberate departures from the netlib text, all three in code paths where the Fortran is wrong:

  • dqc25s accumulates res24 = res12 + cheb24(i)*rg(i) in one loop of its integr = 2 branch, a known typo; the intended res24 + ... is used.
  • dqawfe with ω = 0 integrates over (0, ∞) instead of (a, ∞); a is used.
  • dqagpe’s point sorting and dqawce’s limit swap are not reproduced because Quad hands both routines ordered input; dgtsl’s singular-matrix exits are dropped (the moment systems are never singular).

The Chebyshev moments of QAWO are kept per bisection level as dqc25f keeps them in chebmo/momcom, including the MaxP1 bound and the reuse across QAWF cycles. SciPy 1.18.1’s C translation of QUADPACK mishandles maxp1 smaller than the number of levels and differs from the Fortran on two other fixture integrals; the netlib Fortran, compiled with gfortran, settles each case and this port agrees with it (see verification.md).

Multiple integrals

NQuad nests Quad calls exactly as SciPy’s nquad does (variable 0 innermost, ranges of a variable may depend on the outer ones, per-level options); DblQuad and TplQuad are its two- and three-dimensional forms with SciPy’s argument order (f(y, x), f(z, y, x)). AbsErr is the largest one-dimensional error estimate, as in SciPy; NEval counts integrand calls.

Fixed rules

FixedQuad is the n-point Gauss–Legendre rule (nodes by Newton’s method on the three-term recurrence from Tricomi’s initial guesses, cached per n); Romberg is the removed scipy.integrate.romberg (Richardson extrapolation of the trapezoid rule, DivMax levels); Romb, Trapezoid, CumulativeTrapezoid and Simpson are the sample rules, Simpson with Cartwright’s correction for an even number of samples as SciPy ≥ 1.11. The *ND variants apply them along an axis of an ndarray.Array.

Initial value problems

Common machinery

SolveIVP is SciPy’s solve_ivp loop: one accepted step at a time, events located on the step’s dense output with Brent’s method (SciPy’s brentq, xtol = rtol = 4·eps), TEval filled from the dense output, terminal events counted per event. The initial step is Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner’s (Solving ODEs I, II.4) as select_initial_step computes it; error norms are RMS norms of the error scaled by atol + rtol·|y|; finite-difference Jacobians are SciPy’s num_jac (per-column step factors adapted from call to call, a retry for columns lost in roundoff). Evaluations made for those Jacobians are reported separately (NFevJac), because SciPy does not count them in nfev.

Step-size factors use Python’s built-in max/min semantics, which matter when an estimate is NaN: max(0.2, nan) is 0.2, so a step is rejected and shrunk where math.Max would propagate the NaN. A NaN step size ends the integration with ErrStepTooSmall (SciPy loops forever there, for instance on y₀ = 0 with atol = 0, where every error scale is zero).

Explicit Runge–Kutta: RK45, RK23, DOP853

  • RK45: Dormand & Prince, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 6 (1980); free 4th-order interpolant of Shampine, Math. Comp. 46 (1986).
  • RK23: Bogacki & Shampine, Appl. Math. Lett. 2 (1989), with its cubic Hermite interpolant.
  • DOP853: Hairer’s dop853.f (Hairer, Nørsett & Wanner, Solving ODEs I, II.10): 12 stages, the 5th/3rd-order blended error estimate, the 7th-order dense output with 3 extra stages (evaluated only when a dense output is needed, as SciPy does).

The tableaux are SciPy’s (rk.py, dop853_coefficients.py), which are the published ones; dop853_coefficients.go is generated from them. Step-size control is SciPy’s: safety 0.9, factors in [0.2, 10], no increase right after a rejection.

Radau IIA (order 5)

Hairer & Wanner, Solving ODEs II, IV.8, and radau5.f, as restructured in SciPy’s radau.py: the collocation system is solved by simplified Newton on the transformed variables (one real and one complex LU of μ/h·I - J), at most 6 iterations with the convergence-rate test; the error estimate is radau5’s, refined once after a rejection; the step controller is Gustafsson’s predictive one; the Jacobian is recomputed only when Newton converges slowly. Dense LU with partial pivoting (pivot choice as LAPACK’s getrf, |re|+|im| for the complex matrix) replaces LAPACK.

BDF (orders 1–5)

Shampine & Reichelt, “The MATLAB ODE Suite”, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 18 (1997) — the NDF variant (κ = −0.185, −1/9, −0.0823, −0.0415) — with the quasi-constant step size implementation of SciPy’s bdf.py: a table of backward differences rescaled by change_D whenever the step changes, order selection after order+1 equal steps, at most 4 Newton iterations.

LSODA-style switching (LSODA, Odeint)

ODEPACK’s LSODA (Petzold, SIAM J. Sci. Stat. Comput. 4 (1983)) pairs Adams methods with BDF and switches by comparing the step sizes each would take. This package does not port it. Its LSODA pairs the explicit Dormand–Prince methods (RK45, or DOP853 when the smallest rtol is below 1e-6) with the BDF above:

  • non-stiff → stiff: Hairer’s stiffness detector (Solving ODEs II, IV.2; the test inside dopri5/dop853): after each accepted step hλ ≈ |h|·‖f(y₁) − f(ỹ)‖ / ‖y₁ − ỹ‖ (ỹ the argument of the last internal stage, also at t + h) estimates |h| times the dominant eigenvalue; when it exceeds the stability boundary (3.25 for RK45, 6.1 for DOP853) on 15 steps without 6 consecutive steps below, the step is limited by stability, not accuracy, and BDF takes over with the current step size;
  • stiff → non-stiff: every 5 BDF steps the spectral radius ρ of the Jacobian BDF holds is estimated by power iteration; when |h|·ρ < 1 on two consecutive checks, the explicit method would be stable at BDF’s step size and takes over.

This keeps the property that matters to an odeint user — a stiff problem is not integrated with a stability-limited explicit method, a non-stiff one not with BDF — but its counts differ from ODEPACK’s: on the stiff fixtures it uses 1.2–2.2× the right-hand-side evaluations of ODEPACK (Jacobian columns included on both sides). Odeint reports the method of the last step in MUsed (1 explicit, 2 BDF) with ODEPACK’s meaning.

Interfaces

  • Functions are Go funcs: func(x float64) float64 for integrands, func(t float64, y, dydt []float64) for right-hand sides (the derivative is written, not returned, so steps allocate nothing), and func(t float64, y []float64, jac []float64) for row-major Jacobians.
  • Results are plain slices; Solution.YArray, ODESolution.EvalArray and Odeint return *ndarray.Array in SciPy’s layouts (sol.y is n × len(t), odeint is len(t) × n).
  • Not provided: complex-valued integrands and states, vectorized right-hand sides, sparse Jacobians and jac_sparsity, banded Jacobians and the ml/mu/ixpr/mxordn/mxords/hmin/tcrit options of odeint, quad_vec, cubature, tanhsinh, solve_bvp, and the legacy ode class.
